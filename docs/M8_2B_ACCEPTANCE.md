# Trap Wars V2 - M8.2B Acceptance

## ZERO-BUDGET EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

M8.2B is technically accepted as a zero-budget in-house visual upgrade. No paid procurement or outreach occurred. Hero/Ray identity, phone transition, conversational life, safehouse storytelling, street depth, Hub composition, Day 2 readability, capture tooling, and the clean review build all improved without changing backend or economy behavior.

The result does not honestly reach bespoke 4/5 character or environment art. The CC0 oversized-head meshes, zero facial blend shapes, generic source motion, modular buildings, and primitive vehicles remain visible. Final status: **M8.2B ACCEPTED WITH KNOWN ART CEILING**. M9 remains unauthorized and was not started.

## FREE ASSETS / TOOLS USED

Existing CC0 Quaternius characters/motion, CC0 Kenney environment/audio, Unity Personal 6000.3.20f1 with URP 17.3, one project-specific OpenAI-generated abstract decal atlas, and FFmpeg 7.1 for evidence capture. Total M8.2B spend: $0.

## LICENSES

Quaternius and Kenney remain CC0 with notices retained in Unity art folders. The new abstract decal atlas is project-owned generated output with its prompt and restrictions recorded in `M8_2B_ZERO_BUDGET_ART_PASS.md`. FFmpeg is capture-only and not shipped. No purchased, account-gated, unclear-rights, voice, likeness, or vendor delivery was used.

## HERO CHANGES

Hero now has an original dark crossbody bag/strap and a small interaction response. These improve role silhouette at gameplay distance, but the source head/body proportions and face remain the dominant visual read.

## RAY CHANGES

Ray receives a waist pouch, chain accent, a dressed personal corner, bounded gaze breaks, nod variation, and restrained chest/arm gestures. Ray is easier to distinguish from Hero, though still not a bespoke memorable 4/5 character.

## ANIMATION CHANGES

The existing Humanoid retargeting contract remains intact. M8.2B adds post-pose procedural performance for Ray dialogue and Hero interaction without root-motion or state-authority changes. No authored start/stop/turn/reaction library is claimed.

## PHONE ANIMATION

Two-hand IK now blends in and out over time. The phone prop appears before the draw blend and remains visible until close completes, removing the obvious one-frame pop. This reaches a slice-credible 4/5 behavior even though there is no custom hand-authored draw clip.

## FACIAL FALLBACK

The meshes expose zero facial blend shapes. The accepted fallback uses bounded head aim, occasional attention breaks, and small nod/tilt variation. It is not lip sync, viseme proof, or a production facial rig.

## SAFEHOUSE CHANGES

Added bed hierarchy, textile layers, framed cool window, desk lamp, books, shoes, plan label, additional print, and a clearer desk/work story. The room now feels occupied and decision-oriented; wall/furniture geometry still exposes the procedural base.

## STREET CHANGES

Raised asphalt readability, added facade trim and lit-window rhythm, strengthened route depth, and built a specific Ray corner. The route footprint and interactions are unchanged. Buildings and vehicles remain visibly modular/primitive.

## MATERIAL CHANGES

Added restrained charcoal, timber, linen, emerald, brass/gold, warm-window, and cool-window families plus the new 2048 editorial atlas. Roughness/emission choices improve hierarchy without introducing a large authored material library.

## LIGHTING CHANGES

Day directional intensity and ambient fill were lifted slightly. The Day 2 cool floor was raised to preserve navigation and character separation while retaining warm practical contrast. No baked GI, reflection-probe production pass, or weather lighting was added.

## AUDIO CHANGES

No new M8.2B audio asset was added. The accepted CC0 cue/footstep set remains, and procedural ambience/music remain temporary. No voice was generated or represented as human performance.

## VFX CHANGES

The restrained ACES/bloom/vignette/dust stack was retained. No new effect was necessary for the visual priorities; transaction and transition VFX remain a future polish opportunity.

## CINEMATIC CHANGES

Ray and Hub compositions are clearer through location dressing and revised evidence framing. There is still no authored cinematic camera motion, facial close-up system, or performance edit.

## ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

The safehouse communicates planning, work, cash awareness, and personal routine. Ray's table/ledger/rug makes his street position intentional. The Hub now reads as a late-open neighborhood counter rather than a green interaction block. Original poster graphics add repetition-resistant identity.

## VISUAL COHESION

Charcoal, timber, muted emerald, gold, brick, off-white, and cool teal now repeat across characters, props, signs, and lights. Cohesion improves from M8.1, but the Quaternius character style still conflicts with the grounded environment target.

## THREE BIGGEST BEFORE/AFTER IMPROVEMENTS

1. Hub: visible green proxy block and overlapping signage -> hidden proxy, legible counter, fascia, columns, lights, planters, slats, and one sign hierarchy.
2. Character presentation: instant phone pop and static Ray attention -> eased two-hand phone handling plus bounded dialogue performance and role accessories.
3. Locations: sparse flat safehouse/Ray area -> occupied safehouse, dressed Ray corner, stronger facade rhythm, and original neighborhood graphics.

## THREE REMAINING VISUAL LIMITATIONS

1. Hero/Ray meshes retain oversized heads, simple faces, low-detail hair/clothing, and zero facial shapes.
2. Locomotion/dialogue rely on a small generic CC0 motion set rather than bespoke performance animation.
3. Buildings, vehicles, and major furniture remain modular or primitive and cannot reach bespoke 4/5 environment quality through dressing alone.
